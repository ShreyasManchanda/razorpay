The agent-commerce authorization gap

The agreement is not the evidence.

AP2, ACP, UPI-style mandates, and signed payment instructions can prove the right key authorized the final payload. They cannot prove the negotiation that produced it was honest.

A merchant agent can place an instruction inside its own message history or gradually move the buyer away from its original goal. A valid signature at the end says nothing about that process. Warden evaluates the negotiation before payment execution, at the layer those protocols do not cover.

Same evidence, different tolerance

Detection stays stable. Policy can move.

A consumer quick-commerce policy and a B2B receivables policy can respond differently to the same injection signal. Re-evaluation is a pure function over stored signals: no transcript replay, model call, or new negotiation.

Ambiguity deserves a person

Judgment stays human.

Rejecting every suspicious negotiation would block ordinary price changes, substitutions, and bundles. STEPUP exists for evidence that is concerning enough to pause but not clear enough to reject.

Warden hands a reviewer the change, the affected constraint, the trust crossing, and the reason automation stopped. Approval resumes a disposable checkpoint; the canonical replay remains immutable.

Integration and execution proof

A trust layer agents can call.

Warden sits between an agent-to-agent agreement and Razorpay order creation. Existing agents can submit the intent, signed cart, transcript, and policy through one bounded MCP authorization tool; they receive PASS, STEPUP, or REJECT before money moves.
